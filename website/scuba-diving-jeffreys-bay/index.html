Scuba diving in Jeffreys Bay

Jeffreys Bay is known for its surf, but the reefs offshore are just as good below the surface. Here's what diving here is like, and who to dive with.

What diving in Jeffreys Bay is like

The reefs off Jeffreys Bay and St Francis Bay are temperate volcanic rock, broken up by sand patches, gullies and walls, with hard and soft corals. Expect ragged-tooth sharks (raggies), blue-spotted rays, yellowbelly rockcod, cuttlefish and plenty of nudibranchs. Much of this coast is still lightly dived, so new sites are still being found.

Most dives here are boat dives that launch through the surf from Main Beach, about 1 km from Greystone. Launches depend on the sea, so dive days are planned around the forecast rather than fixed in advance.

Our local dive centre

Dive with J-Bay SCUBA

J-Bay SCUBA is run by Paul van Jaarsveld, owner, operator and instructor, who has dived around Jeffreys Bay and St Francis Bay almost daily for more than a decade and served as an NSRI sea rescue coxswain. They train through RAID and work with NSRI, SAMSA and DAN.

  • Try Dive. A first taste of scuba in the pool, with the option of an ocean dive. Open from age 8; under-18s need a parent's consent.
  • Courses. RAID Open Water, Explorer 30, Advanced 35 and Nitrox, with junior versions for younger divers, plus a Reboot refresher if you haven't dived in a while.
  • Boat dives and charters. Single or double dives on the local reefs, for groups or private charters.
  • Gear. Equipment rental, air fills and servicing, so you don't need to travel with your kit.
  • More on the water. Snorkelling, ocean safaris, Gamtoos River safaris, sailing and spearfishing.

See current courses and prices on the J-Bay SCUBA training page, check the dive forecast, or WhatsApp Paul on 083 521 5093.

A few of the local reefs

Dive sites off Jeffreys Bay

J-Bay SCUBA dives around 20 named sites. A few of them, from shallow to deep:

7–10 m

Liza's Raggie

Gullies of sea grass and red bait, busy with nudibranchs, anemones and double-sash butterflyfish.

12 m

Discovery Channel

A shallow reef with good light for photos, and something new on most dives.

14 m

Raggies Rest

Named for the groups of ragged-tooth sharks, 2 to 2.5 m long, that gather here.

16 m

Rock Cod

Wide sandy gullies and plenty of yellowbelly rockcod.

20 m

Namakwa Reef

A colourful "flower pot" reef on Long Mile Reef, further east off J-Bay.

35–37 m

Pramberg Reef

A deep reef about 8 km out by boat, with large reef patches and white sand.

More sites and photos are on J-Bay SCUBA's dive sites page.

Staying at Greystone on a dive trip

Greystone is in Wavecrest, 260 m from the beach and a short drive from the Main Beach launch site. There's free on-site parking for your car and gear, and a cooked breakfast is available to order before an early launch. Solar power with battery backup keeps the lights and WiFi on during load-shedding, so you can still check the forecast.
