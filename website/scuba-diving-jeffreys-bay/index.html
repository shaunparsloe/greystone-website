Below the surf at J-Bay

Scuba diving in Jeffreys Bay

Jeffreys Bay is famous for what happens on top of the water. Underneath, the reefs are full of life and hardly dived. Here's what to expect, how a dive day works, and who to dive with.

Mostly undiscovered

What's on the reefs

The reefs off Jeffreys Bay and St Francis Bay are temperate volcanic rock: walls, ledges and sandy gullies, covered in hard and soft corals and sponges. It's not a tropical coral reef, and it's not crowded either. Very few people dive this stretch of the Eastern Cape, so new sites are still being found, and divers occasionally spot species nobody has named yet.

The regulars include:

  • Ragged-tooth sharks ("raggies"), slow and mellow, often resting in the gullies
  • Blue-spotted rays on the sand
  • Yellowbelly rockcod, sometimes in big numbers
  • Cuttlefish, nudibranchs and anemones for macro fans

Dive sites to know

J-Bay SCUBA has around twenty named sites off Jeffreys Bay and Cape St Francis, from shallow gullies for a first ocean dive to deep reefs for advanced divers. A few of them:

7–10 m

Liza's Raggie

Shallow gullies with sea grass and red bait, lots of nudibranchs and anemones.

12 m

Discovery Channel

Bright, shallow reef that's good for photos.

14 m

Raggies Rest

Ragged-tooth sharks of 2–2.5 m resting in the gullies.

16 m

Rock Cod

Wide sandy gullies and plenty of yellowbelly rockcod.

16 m

Jagged Edge

Gullies up to 30 m wide, good for practising navigation.

20 m

Namakwa Reef

A very colourful pinnacle on Long Mile Reef.

25 m

Moonrock Reef

A newer find: flat, unspoilt reef with lots of small life.

35–37 m

Pramberg Reef

A deep reef about 8 km offshore, for advanced divers.

Descriptions and photos of every site are on J-Bay SCUBA's dive sites page. Which one you dive on the day depends on the conditions and on your experience.

How a dive day works

Diving here is boat diving. The boat surf-launches from Jeffreys Bay Main Beach, about 2 km from Greystone, and in season also runs from Port St Francis. It takes up to six divers plus the skipper, and the skippers are experienced, rescue-trained coxswains. You can do a single dive or a double dive with two cylinders on one launch.

Days follow the sea, not a fixed timetable. Swell and wind decide whether the boat goes out, so dives are confirmed close to the day, weekdays included. Two tips:

  • Watch the forecast. J-Bay SCUBA's dive forecast rates each day of the coming week as sublime, still good or not good for diving.
  • Leave spare days. If you're coming to dive, book a few nights rather than one, so a windy day doesn't cost you the trip. J-Bay SCUBA can add you to their WhatsApp group, where launches are announced.

The local dive centre

Diving with J-Bay SCUBA

J-Bay SCUBA is the dive centre in Jeffreys Bay. It's run by Paul van Jaarsveld, owner, operator and instructor, who has dived these reefs almost daily for more than a decade and was a coxswain at the local NSRI sea rescue station for 12 years. Training is through RAID, in small, unhurried groups.

  • Try Dive: a first go at scuba with an instructor in the pool, with the option of a guided ocean dive. No certification needed; children can start in the pool from age 8.
  • Open Water: the full certification, done as online study plus at least three to four days of pool and ocean dives. Junior Open Water is for ages 10–14.
  • Next steps: Explorer 30, Advanced 35, Nitrox, Deep 40 and rescue courses, plus a Reboot refresher if you haven't dived for a while.
  • Boat dives and charters: group launches for certified divers, or a private double dive with a skipper and guide for one or two people.
  • Gear: equipment rental and air fills, so you can travel light.
  • Not diving? Snorkelling, ocean safaris, Gamtoos River safaris and spearfishing.

Course details and fees are on the training page, and boat dive and rental prices on the SCUBA charters page. To book, WhatsApp Paul on 083 521 5093.

Planning your dive trip

  • Getting here: fly into Chief Dawid Stuurman International Airport in Port Elizabeth (Gqeberha), 64 km away, about an hour's drive. A car is useful for getting to the launch with your gear.
  • Learning to dive: allow about a week for Open Water. You'll need three to four good-weather days, and you can do the online study before you arrive.
  • Already certified: bring your certification card and logbook. If you're out of practice, ask about the Reboot refresher first.
  • Flying home: leave at least 24 hours between your last dive and your flight.

Diving questions

Can you scuba dive in Jeffreys Bay?

Yes. The local dive centre, J-Bay SCUBA, runs boat dives on the reefs off Jeffreys Bay, surf-launching from Main Beach, and seasonally from Port St Francis. They also run RAID courses, from a first Try Dive to Open Water and beyond.

Do I need to be a certified diver?

Not to try it. A Try Dive needs no certification: you learn the basics in the pool with an instructor, with the option of a guided ocean dive. To dive on your own with a buddy you need an Open Water certification, which J-Bay SCUBA teaches over at least three to four days of practical training.

What will I see diving in Jeffreys Bay?

Temperate rocky reefs with gullies, walls, hard and soft corals and sponges. Ragged-tooth sharks, blue-spotted rays, yellowbelly rockcod, cuttlefish and nudibranchs are regulars, and few people dive here, so new sites are still being found.

How far is Greystone from the dive launch?

J-Bay SCUBA's boats launch from Main Beach, about 2 km from Greystone, around five minutes by car. We have free private parking on site, so you can load your gear at the door.

When is the best time to dive Jeffreys Bay?

The reefs are busy with life all year. Dive days depend on the swell and wind rather than the calendar, so check J-Bay SCUBA's dive forecast and leave a spare day or two in your trip in case the sea isn't right.

Can I rinse and store my dive gear at Greystone?

Please ask Monique when you book, so she can tell you what's possible for your room.
