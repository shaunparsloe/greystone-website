Jan se Gat Waterfall

Had enough sand for one week? Just outside Jeffreys Bay, the Kabeljous River drops over a rock ledge into a deep, dark swimming hole. Here's how to get there, what to expect and how to stay safe.

A local swimming hole

A river pool with a cliff jump

Locals call it Jan se Gat (you'll also see Jan se Watergat), and on a hot weekend it's where they go to swim, jump off the rocks and bake in the sun. Give it half a day.

It's on a private farm beside the Kabeljous Nature Reserve, and you reach it on foot through bush and fynbos. At the end of the walk there's a small zipline, a slide and a 12 m cliff jump. Students and young adults love it. So do families, as long as the kids are old enough to swim properly.

Getting there

The farm has no street address, so don't bother with one. Type "Jan se Gat Waterfall" into Google Maps and follow its directions. You'll end up on the Kabeljous River near Smithtown, roughly 7 km north of Greystone as the crow flies.

From Wavecrest it's a short drive, and most people leave the car along the access road. If you'd rather not drive, some tour operators run a waterfall day trip with pickup in Jeffreys Bay.

Before you set off, message the farm on 083 230 7790 (phone or WhatsApp) to check opening times and fees.

What to expect

Plan on two to three hours, walk included. The path winds through bushveld and fynbos, and parts of it are rough and have no shade at all. Leave the pram and the wheelchair at home; it won't work for either.

Then you reach the water. The pool is deep in places, with flat rocks to sit on and a few trees for shade. As for the fun stuff:

  • Zipline: short and about 5 m up, and it ends with you in the pool.
  • Slide: a foefie slide into the water. Gentler than jumping, and a good warm-up.
  • Cliff jump: about 12 m down into the pond. Confident swimmers only, and read the safety section first.

Don't expect a kiosk or anything much in the way of facilities, because there aren't any. Pack your own food, drinks and camp chairs.

When to go

Weekends only. Every listing we found says it's open Saturday and Sunday and shut during the week, but the hours they give don't quite agree, and public holidays can change things too. A quick WhatsApp beforehand saves a wasted trip.

Get there soon after the gate opens if you can. You'll walk in before the path turns into an oven, and you'll have the pool mostly to yourself for a while before the crowds arrive.

Summer (November to March) gives you the warmest water, along with the most people and a brutal midday sun. The waterfall itself looks its best after decent rain. But if the river is in flood or the water's turned murky, stay out of it. Don't swim and don't jump.

Read this before you jump

Tips and safety

Nobody's watching over you here. Jan se Gat is a wild river pool on private land, with no lifeguards and no formal rules. In February 2022 a young man died after jumping from a ledge; he probably struck his head on a rock before he hit the water (Algoa FM).

So if you're going to jump, watch where the locals land and check the depth yourself before you go anywhere near the edge. Never jump alone, never after a few drinks, and never into water you can't see into. Keep a close eye on children as well. Some spots are deep and the rocks get slippery.

A few practical things:

  • Cash: there's sometimes a small entrance fee, depending on the time of year.
  • Your bag: lots of drinking water, a hat, high-SPF sunscreen, closed shoes for the walk, water shoes for the rocks, a towel, food and a chair.
  • Rubbish: it's someone's farm. Take everything home with you and stick to the landowner's rules.

Make a day of it

The waterfall only fills a morning or an afternoon, so pair it with something else on the north side of town. Right next door, the Kabeljous Nature Reserve has coastal trails and about 100 bird species. At Papiesfontein you can do a 13 km horse ride that finishes along a quiet stretch of beach, or try a guided sandboarding session on the dunes near Marina Martinique.

Back in town, wander past Supertubes, look in at the Jeffreys Bay Shell Museum (it holds more than 600 species), or end the day with a craft beer at Brewhaha. If you've got a spare full day, Addo Elephant Park is close enough for a trip from Jeffreys Bay. There's more in our guide to things to do in Jeffreys Bay and our restaurant guide.

Waterfall questions

Where is Jan se Gat Waterfall?

On the Kabeljous River near Smithtown, on a private farm beside the Kabeljous Nature Reserve, roughly 7 km north of Greystone as the crow flies. It has no street address: search for "Jan se Gat Waterfall" in Google Maps and follow its directions.

When is Jan se Gat open?

Weekends only, according to every listing we found, and closed during the week. The listed hours don't agree and public holidays can change things, so WhatsApp the farm on 083 230 7790 before you go to check times and the entrance fee.

Is it safe to jump at Jan se Gat?

Only if you take care. There are no lifeguards, and a young man died after jumping from a ledge in 2022. Check the depth yourself, watch where the locals land, and never jump alone, after drinking, into murky water or when the river is in flood.

What should I bring to Jan se Gat?

Plenty of drinking water, a hat, high-SPF sunscreen, closed shoes for the walk, water shoes for the rocks, a towel, food, a camp chair and some cash for the entrance fee. There's no kiosk, and you should take all your rubbish home.
